New to options?

Think of it as renting out a stock you like.

An option is a contract on 100 shares of a stock — the right to buy or sell at a set price by a set date. Most people who land here aren't chasing a lottery ticket; they want a steady bit of extra income from stocks they already own or watch. The strategies marked 💰 Income in the library do exactly that: you collect a payment up front, and your only job is to understand what happens to that payment at different prices — which is exactly what this page shows you before you risk a dollar.

Strike price
The price you're locked into buying or selling at, no matter where the stock actually trades.
Premium
What you pay (or get paid) for the contract — your cost, or your income, depending on the trade.
Breakeven
The stock price where the trade stops losing and starts winning. Below shows one, or a range.

IV Rank & Delta — quick reference

Two numbers experienced options traders lean on constantly. Neither is required to use OptionsBuddy — they just help decide which strategy fits the moment.

IV Rank 0–25
Options are cheap versus their own recent history. Favors buying premium — long calls/puts, long straddles/strangles.
IV Rank 25–50
Middle ground. Either side can work — lean on your directional read.
IV Rank 50–75
Getting rich. Favors selling premium — credit spreads, iron condors, covered calls.
IV Rank 75–100
Options are expensive, often around news or earnings. Strong case for selling premium, sized carefully.
~10–16Δ
Far out-of-the-money. Low odds of finishing ITM — used for wide, low-premium wings.
~16–30Δ
The classic credit-spread zone — roughly a 70–84% chance of expiring out-of-the-money, balanced against the premium collected.
~40–60Δ
Close to the money. Higher premium and higher odds of being tested — typical for directional debit trades.
~100Δ
Behaves just like the stock itself (e.g. a deep ITM call, or 100 shares).
